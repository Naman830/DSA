## 1. Read this first (the TL;DR)

Your main event is **Summer 2028 internships** (the summer after your 3rd year). Applications for those open **July–December 2027**. That gives you roughly **12 months to become genuinely strong, and 4–5 months after that to interview**. This is enough time — comfortably — at 7 hours a day.

You also have **one early lottery ticket: Google STEP India**, which historically opens applications around **September–November** for 1st/2nd-year students. You're a 2nd-year now, so **the 2026 window (for Summer 2027) is your only chance at STEP**. Apply the day it opens even though you won't feel ready. It costs nothing, STEP interviews are easier than regular SWE intern interviews (LeetCode easy/medium level), and even getting an interview is free experience.

The one-line strategy for a tier-3 student:

> **DSA is the gatekeeper. Proof-of-work is the differentiator. Referrals are the multiplier.**

- **Gatekeeper**: no amount of projects gets you past interviews if you can't solve two LeetCode mediums in an hour. DSA gets ~70% of your prep time until you're strong.
- **Differentiator**: your resume must survive a 10-second screen without a brand-name college on it. Contest ratings, a real internship, open source (GSoC), and 1–2 serious projects do that.
- **Multiplier**: off-campus applications into a black hole convert at ~1–2%. The same application with a referral converts several times better. You will build a referral pipeline deliberately, not hope for one.

Three decisions, made now, so you never re-litigate them:

1. **Learn C++ for DSA.** Keep JavaScript for development work. (Why: the entire Indian prep + competitive programming ecosystem runs on C++, it's fastest in contests, and Codeforces JS support is second-class. The 2–3 week switching cost is cheapest today.)
2. **Stop collecting web-dev tutorials.** No new frameworks, no new courses. MERN is now an *asset you deploy* (projects, startup internships), not a subject you study.
3. **Calendar beats mood.** You apply when applications open, not when you feel ready. You study the planned topic today, not the shiny one.

## 3. What big tech actually evaluates

The funnel for an SWE intern role, and what each stage filters on:

1. **Resume screen (seconds, often automated).** Filters on: internship/work experience, achievements with numbers (ratings, ranks, selections like GSoC), projects that sound real, CGPA above cutoff (usually 7.0–7.5; keep **8.0+** to be safe). Kills: walls of text, 10 CRUD clones, certificate lists.
2. **Online Assessment (OA).** 1–3 DSA problems in 60–105 min (typically LeetCode easy→medium, occasionally hard at Google), sometimes CS-fundamentals MCQs, sometimes a work-style survey (Amazon — answer honestly and consistently). Filters on: can you actually solve problems fast, alone, under time.
3. **Technical interviews (1–3 rounds, 45–60 min each).** One or two DSA problems solved live while explaining your thinking; follow-ups on complexity and edge cases; often a project walkthrough; sometimes OS/DBMS/OOP basics. Filters on: problem-solving process, code quality, communication, honesty when stuck.
4. **Behavioral / HR (sometimes merged into tech rounds).** Amazon runs Leadership Principles questions; Google evaluates "Googleyness". Filters on: teamwork stories, ownership, curiosity. Prepared STAR stories win here.

**What they do NOT need from an intern:** system design (HLD), Kubernetes, 15 frameworks, paid certificates, research papers. Interns are hired on **problem-solving ability + evidence you can code + signs you'll grow**. That's the whole game.

## 5. The phases in detail

### Phase 0 · Setup (this week, ~5 days)

- [ ] Install a C++ toolchain + VS Code. Learn C++ basics *as a JS developer*: syntax, STL (`vector`, `string`, `map`, `unordered_map`, `set`, `priority_queue`, `pair`, `sort`), references, and I/O. You are not "learning programming" — you already program. 4–5 focused days is enough to start solving; fluency comes by solving.
- [ ] Accounts: LeetCode, Codeforces, GitHub (clean up: real name, photo, pin best 2 repos), LinkedIn (headline: "SWE Intern aspirant · Full-stack (MERN) · C++/DSA" — will evolve).
- [ ] Make **one tracker spreadsheet** (see §18). Every problem you fail goes in it with a re-solve date.
- [ ] Resume v1 using **Jake's Resume template** (search "Jake Gutierrez resume Overleaf"). One page. It will be weak. That's fine — you need a baseline to improve and something to send if STEP opens early.
- [ ] Bookmark: Striver's A2Z DSA Course sheet (takeuforward.org) — this is your spine. Secondary: NeetCode 150 (pattern revision later).

### Phase 1 · Foundations (Sep–Nov 2026)

**Goal:** finish core linear + tree DSA. **~160 new problems** (≈ 1.5–2/day plus learning time). Exit at ~260 total lifetime problems.

Topic order (Striver A2Z order, roughly):
1. Time/space complexity, arrays, hashing (Sep wk 1–2)
2. Two pointers, sliding window, prefix sums (Sep wk 3)
3. Binary search — on arrays and *on answers* (Sep wk 4)
4. Sorting, strings (Oct wk 1)
5. Linked lists, stacks, queues, monotonic stack (Oct wk 2–3)
6. Recursion + backtracking (Oct wk 4 — do not rush this; it's the foundation for trees and DP)
7. Binary trees, BSTs — all traversals, views, LCA, diameter (Nov wk 1–3)
8. Heaps / priority queue, intervals, greedy intro (Nov wk 4)

**In parallel:**
- **Google STEP**: check weekly from Sep 1; apply immediately when live (resume + essays — write honestly about your MERN work and why CS). If you get an OA/interview, pause new topics for 2 weeks and grind easy/medium arrays-strings-hashmaps + practice explaining aloud.
- From **October**: LeetCode Weekly Contest every Sunday (8:00 AM IST). Your rating will start low. Irrelevant — you're building the habit and the nerve.
- Dev: **1 hr/day max** — start upgrading your best MERN project (§9). No new tutorials.

**Exit criteria:** you can solve an unseen LeetCode easy in ~10–15 min and about half of unseen mediums on core topics in ~35–40 min.

### Phase 2 · Power-up (Dec 2026–Mar 2027)

**Goal:** the hard 40% of DSA — graphs and DP — plus contest habit and open-source entry. Exit at ~380–400 total problems.

1. **Graphs** (Dec): BFS/DFS, connected components, cycle detection, topological sort, shortest paths (Dijkstra, Bellman-Ford, Floyd-Warshall), DSU, MST. December vacation = sprint month (9–10 hrs/day): graphs can be *finished* here.
2. **Dynamic programming** (Jan–Feb): 1D, grids, subsequences (LCS family), knapsack family, strings, DP on trees, LIS. This is the topic that separates candidates. Budget 6 full weeks; solve 80–100 DP problems.
3. **Remaining**: tries, bit manipulation, math/number theory basics, advanced greedy (Mar).

**In parallel:**
- **Codeforces from Dec**: 2 rated contests/week + *upsolve* (after every contest, solve the first problem you couldn't). Start of a rating you'll put on your resume.
- **Open source from Dec**: pick 1–2 orgs from last year's GSoC list whose stack you know (plenty use JS/TS — you have an edge). Set up the project locally, fix `good first issue`s, become known in their chat. By Feb you'll know if GSoC 2027 is realistic.
- **GSoC proposal (late Mar 2027)**: only if you have merged PRs and org familiarity by then. If not, skip without guilt and double down on DSA — GSoC is a multiplier, not a requirement.

**Exit criteria:** unseen LC medium ≈ 60–70% solve rate in ~30 min. Codeforces ~Pupil, pushing Specialist.

### Phase 3 · Proof-of-work summer (Apr–Jul 2027)

This summer must produce **one resume headline**. In priority order (pick what materializes):

1. **GSoC selected** → do it excellently. This is the single strongest tier-3 equalizer for global targets.
2. **Paid startup internship** (apply Mar–Apr on Internshala, Wellfound, Cuvette, Unstop + cold DMs to founders of small startups using MERN — your stack is exactly what they hire interns for). Even ₹10–15k/month is irrelevant; the line on the resume and the production experience are the point.
3. **Flagship project** (§9) if neither lands — built like a job, 6 weeks, shipped with users.

**In parallel (non-negotiable even during internship):**
- **NeetCode 150 second pass** — re-solve for speed and pattern recall; company-tagged lists begin (LeetCode premium for 2–3 months around now is the *one* purchase worth considering).
- **CS fundamentals** (§10): OS + DBMS/SQL in Apr–May, CN + OOP in Jun–Jul, 1 hr/day.
- **Mock interviews begin (June)**: 1/week — Pramp/peers/seniors. Practice the *performance*: think aloud, clarify, test.
- **Flipkart GRiD (~Jun)**: register, do the quiz rounds. Free shot at a PPI.
- **July: referral pipeline construction begins** (§12) — connect with 10–15 engineers/alumni per target company *before* season opens, so you're not a stranger in August.
- Resume v3, reviewed by 3+ people (seniors who cracked offers, r/EngineeringResumes, Discord/Telegram communities like "Grind" groups).

### Phase 4 · Application season (Aug 2027–Jan 2028)

The season you've built toward. Mode switch: **maintenance + performance**, not learning.

- **Apply to everything you're eligible for, the day it opens.** Big tech + quant + fintech + tier-2 product + funded startups. Target: **100+ applications**. Set job alerts (LinkedIn, company career pages, Unstop); check twice a week on a fixed schedule.
- **Every application gets a referral attempt first** (§12). Order of operations: find the job ID → message 2–3 warm contacts at that company → referred application → cold application only if no response in ~5 days.
- **Practice mix**: daily 2 timed problems (one company-tagged), 1 mock/week minimum, keep one weekly contest for sharpness.
- **Behavioral prep**: write 6 STAR stories (a hard bug, a team conflict, a deadline, a thing you learned fast, a project you led, a failure). Amazon *will* ask these; everyone else benefits too.
- **Before every OA**: 2–3 timed practice sets on that company's recent question style (LeetCode discuss/company tags). Never attempt an OA cold or tired; they're often one-shot.
- **ICPC (optional, ~Oct–Dec)**: if CF rating is 1400+, form a team and attempt prelims. A regionals appearance is a strong resume line; don't force it otherwise.

### Phase 5 · Close it out (Feb–Apr 2028)

- Off-cycle and late openings keep appearing (startups + some big tech backfills) — keep the alert habit.
- If a big-tech offer hasn't landed: take the **best startup internship** you can get for Summer 2028 and run this playbook again for **new-grad roles and 6-month internship cycles in your final year** — with a far stronger resume. This path (startup intern → big-tech new-grad or 1–2 YOE lateral) is extremely common and only ~1 year "slower."
- Once an offer lands: learn their stack basics, Git deeply, and rest. Interns convert to PPO on: shipping their project, asking good questions, and being easy to work with.

## 14. "Am I ready?" — the checklist and the truth

**The truth first: readiness is a calendar event, not a feeling.** You will never feel ready — everyone from tier-3 *and* from IIT feels like a fraud the night before an OA. You apply on schedule, to everything, and let *their* process decide. Applications are free, rejections are silent, and every OA is paid practice (paid in experience, by them). The only readiness question that matters is "have I done today's work?"

## 15. Interview prep (how each round is actually won)

**OAs:**
- Simulate before every real one: same duration, no music, no phone.
- Read constraints first — they tell you the intended complexity (n ≤ 10⁵ → O(n log n), n ≤ 20 → bitmask/backtracking).
- Partial credit is real: brute-force what you can't optimize.
- **Never cheat** (ChatGPT/friend during OA). Proctoring catches much of it, blacklists are shared, and an interview instantly exposes a faked OA anyway.

**Live technical rounds — run the same loop every time:**
1. Restate the problem + confirm with an example you make up.
2. Ask 2–3 clarifying questions (empty input? duplicates? size limits?).
3. Say the brute force + its complexity *out loud*, then improve it. Never dive silently into code.
4. Get interviewer buy-in ("I'm thinking a heap keyed on X — shall I code that?").
5. Code cleanly (real variable names), narrating.
6. Test aloud on the example + one edge case, *before* they ask. Fix bugs calmly — they're watching recovery, not perfection.
7. Stuck? Say what you know, what you're missing, and take the hint gracefully — hint-usage done well is a *positive* signal.

**Behavioral:** 6 STAR stories (situation-task-action-result), each ≤ 2 minutes, each with a number in the result. Amazon: map stories to Leadership Principles (Ownership, Dive Deep, Learn & Be Curious especially). Be specific and honest; interviewers pattern-match rehearsed fluff instantly.

**Mocks are the multiplier**: from Jun 2027, 1/week (peers, seniors, Pramp). The gap between "can solve" and "can solve while a stranger watches" is closed only this way.

## 16. FAQ — every doubt, answered straight

**Q1. Is my tier-3 college a dealbreaker?**
No — it's a resume-screen tax you pay off with ratings, internships, GSoC, and referrals. Thousands of tier-3 students enter big tech every year, overwhelmingly off-campus. It costs you extra applications (send 100+, not 15), not the outcome.

**Q2. What CGPA do I need?**
Keep **8.0+** and stop optimizing beyond that. Most cutoffs are 7.0–7.5; below 7 silently kills applications. An hour that takes you from 8.1→8.4 is worth less than that hour in DSA; an hour that keeps you above 7.5 is worth more.

**Q3. Should I drop web dev completely for DSA?**
No — cap it at ~1 hr/day and change its purpose: MERN is now for *shipping proof-of-work* (projects, OSS, startup internships), not for learning. Zero new frameworks/courses. DSA gets the prime hours because it's the only gate every company checks.

**Q4. C++, Java, Python, or my JavaScript for DSA?**
**C++.** The Indian prep ecosystem (Striver, editorials, CP) is C++-first, it's fastest for contests, and Codeforces JS support is poor. Java/Python are acceptable; JS is the weakest choice here despite your comfort. Interviewers don't care which you use — pick once, never switch again. Your JS stays for dev work.

**Q5. Do I need a paid course?**
No. Striver A2Z + NeetCode + LeetCode + YouTube CS playlists cover 100%. The only purchase worth considering ever: LeetCode Premium for 2–3 months before season (company tags). Anyone selling you a "placement guarantee" course is selling to your anxiety.

**Q6. Do certificates (Udemy/Coursera/NPTEL) help my resume?**
Essentially zero weight at big tech. Delete the certificate section; fill Achievements with ratings/ranks/selections instead. (Exception: keep learning from courses if useful — just don't *list* them as credentials.)

**Q7. How many LeetCode problems is enough?**
~500 *quality* solves with spaced re-solving beats 1,500 copied ones. The metric that matters isn't count — it's your hit rate on *unseen* mediums under time. Track that monthly.

**Q8. Are contests compulsory?**
For you, effectively yes: rating is the cheapest credential a tier-3 resume can carry, quant firms filter on it, and contests train the exact timed-solo-solving that OAs test. 2–3 per week is enough; you're not required to become red.

**Q9. What do I do when I can't solve a problem?**
40 minutes of honest effort → read the *full* editorial → close it → re-implement from memory → schedule re-solves at +7 and +30 days. Struggling *before* the editorial is where learning happens; skipping the re-implementation is where it dies.

**Q10. Do internship interviews ask system design?**
Almost never. OOP/LLD basics occasionally. Skip HLD guilt-free until final year — anyone telling a 2nd-year to study Kafka for internships is wasting your hours.

**Q11. Is GSoC worth the time vs more DSA?**
If (and only if) you have merged PRs by Feb 2027, yes — it's the strongest global brand-equalizer available to you and pays a stipend. If OSS hasn't clicked by then, skip the proposal without guilt; DSA + a startup internship covers you domestically.

**Q12. When EXACTLY do I apply?**
STEP: Sep–Nov 2026 (now-ish). Startup internships: Mar–Apr 2027 for summer, or any winter. Main season: **the day each opening goes live, Aug 2027–Jan 2028**, referral attempt first, 100+ total. Speed matters — most pipelines are rolling.

**Q13. When will I know I'm ready?**
Wrong question — see §14. You apply by calendar. The measurable bar (2 unseen mediums/hour) is for *calibrating prep*, not for permission to apply.

**Q14. How do I get a referral when I know nobody?**
You manufacture the network: alumni search on LinkedIn (your college → company), engineers posting "happy to refer", people in your OSS org. Start July 2027 so August-you isn't a stranger. §12 has the exact templates and etiquette. Expect ~1 referral per 5–8 polite asks — it's a numbers game with manners.

**Q15. What about hackathons?**
1–2 serious ones (Flipkart GRiD, Smart India Hackathon, big company-run events on Unstop) for the resume line and PPI chances. Don't become a hackathon tourist collecting participation swag — it eats DSA weekends.

**Q16. My English/communication is shaky. How much does it matter?**
It's scored in every round. Fix it cheaply: narrate one solution aloud daily from Phase 2, do mocks in English from Jun 2027, watch your own recordings. You need clarity, not accent or vocabulary.

**Q17. Can I use AI (ChatGPT/Copilot) while prepping?**
For *learning* (explain this editorial, review my code) — great. For *solving your daily problems* — poison; the interview room has no AI. And never in an OA (§15).

**Q18. 7+ hours daily — how do I not burn out?**
One full evening off weekly, 7+ hours of sleep (memory consolidation IS study), exercise 30 min/day, exam-week minimum of 1 problem/day, and never "compensate" a missed day with a 14-hour binge. You're running a 20-month campaign; the person who shows up daily beats the person who sprints monthly.

**Q19. What if I do everything and still don't get a big-tech internship?**
Then in Summer 2028 you intern at the best startup that'll have you, and you re-run the playbook for final-year 6-month internships and new-grad roles with a much stronger resume — plus the extremely common 1–2-years-experience lateral into big tech. This roadmap's skills are permanent; the 2028 date is just the first attempt window, not the verdict.

**Q20. Should I also apply to service companies (TCS/Infosys/Wipro)?**
Not during internship season — they mostly hire final-years and would cap your prep signal. Keep them as final-year safety nets only.

**Q21. Do big tech internships pay? (Am I aiming for something real?)**
Yes — big tech India SWE intern stipends are typically ~₹60k–₹1.2L+/month, quant firms often multiples of that, and the real prize is the **PPO** (pre-placement offer) that skips the entire placement grind.

**Q22. On-campus placement cell — ignore it?**
No — register for everything it offers (some product companies do visit tier-3 campuses via pooled drives), but *plan* as if it gives you nothing. Anything it delivers is a bonus.

**Q23. Girls-only programs, diversity programs?**
Not applicable to you, but know the category exists so you're not confused seeing peers get earlier interviews (Amazon WOW, Adobe women programs, etc. run on separate calendars).

**Q24. Should I do a "6-month internship" in 3rd year if offered?**
If it's a strong product company and your college permits the semester away — usually yes; it often converts and outweighs coursework. Decide case-by-case with the PPO likelihood as the deciding factor.

**Q25. One thing people in my position most commonly get wrong?**
Tutorial-hopping and plan-shopping — switching sheets/courses/roadmaps every month, which feels like progress and compounds nothing. You now have one plan. The entire game is boring, daily execution of it for 12 months. The plan doesn't crack the internship; the 2,500 hours do.
